# Tool Intent Statement: Mood Compass

## 1. Core Function
A daily mood journal that lets users record how they feel, identify patterns over time, and receive gentle encouragement on difficult days. The tool doesn't diagnose or judge. It observes alongside the user, reflects patterns back, and offers a quiet reminder that difficult moods pass—and that consistency matters.

## 2. Human Context
People live inside their moods but rarely step back to see them. "I've been sad all month" versus "I had 18 difficult days and 12 good ones"—the second is more true, but the first *feels* true. Mood feels like a fixed state until you write it down and see the pattern. 

On hard days, it's easy to believe nothing helps. But when a streak breaks or a pattern shifts, it's proof that things change. A mood journal can be that proof. Not therapy. Not diagnosis. Just: here's what you felt, here's what shifted, and—look—you kept showing up.

## 3. Intended User
Sarah, 31, struggles with mood variability. Some days she feels capable; others, heavy. She doesn't know if her moods follow patterns or if they're just random. She wants to understand what influences her, and on the bad days, she wants something that reminds her: this too will shift. She's not looking for a cure. She's looking for clarity and kindness.

## 4. Behavioral Thesis
**Awareness transforms invisibility into pattern.** When moods become data (not judgment), the user can see: "I'm consistently lower on Mondays" or "Exercise days have different curves." This creates agency. And when someone has a difficult day but sees they maintained a 12-day streak, the streak becomes proof of resilience—not a thing to feel ashamed about breaking.

The thesis: **Patterns are real. Streaks are proof. Difficult moods are temporary.**

## 5. Dignity Clause
This tool respects the user by:
- **Never suggesting the user should feel differently.** No toxic positivity. No "just think happy thoughts." Bad days are valid.
- **Never creating pressure.** A broken streak is not failure. The user can start again immediately.
- **Never pathologizing normal mood variation.** This is not a mental health diagnosis tool. It's a journal.
- **Never comparing moods to others.** No social features. No "you're sadder than average." Private observation only.
- **Never exploiting mood data.** No ads targeted by emotional state. No algorithmic recommendations based on sadness.
- **Being honest about limitations.** If patterns suggest clinical concern, the tool gently suggests professional support—not as shame, but as care.

## 6. Refusal Clause
This tool will intentionally NOT:
- Suggest that mood is "fixable" through the app alone
- Compare the user's moods to others or to averages
- Create social sharing or competition
- Use mood data for personalization or advertising
- Judge any mood as "wrong" or "bad"
- Offer unsolicited advice (e.g., "You're sad, try exercise")
- Become a replacement for professional mental health support
- Include "motivation" notifications or gamification that feels hollow

## 7. Atmospheric Tone
**Gentle. Non-judgmental. Honest.**

The interface should feel like a trusted notebook—something you can be honest with. Colors might shift subtly based on mood (not as judgment, but as reflection). The language is never cheerful or clinical. It's simply: "How are you feeling today?"

When showing patterns, the tone is observational, not prescriptive: "You tend to feel lower on mornings after late sleep" rather than "You should sleep earlier."

On difficult days, when the user logs a hard mood, the tone should be: you're not alone in this, you showed up to notice, and this moment will pass.

---

## Design Principle
**A mood journal should be a mirror, not a judge. A witness, not a solution. A quiet space that says: I see you, your feelings are real, and you're not the only one who struggles.**

Design for that.
