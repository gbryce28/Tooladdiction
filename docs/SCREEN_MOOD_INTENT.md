# Tool Intent Statement: Screen & Mood Correlation

## 1. Core Function
A daily reflection tool with three steps: (1) Rate your day's mood 1-5, (2) Log screen time hours, (3) View a graph showing the correlation between your mood and screen usage over time. The tool makes visible what users often suspect but never confirm: *how screens actually affect how you feel.*

## 2. Human Context
People scroll without noticing. Hours pass. They look up and feel hollow. But the connection between *what they did* and *how they feel* is invisible—abstract. "I was on my phone all day" and "I feel terrible" seem unrelated until you see them on the same graph. Then it becomes undeniable: this behavior produces this feeling.

This tool doesn't judge the screen time. It simply makes the relationship visible. Once visible, users can decide.

## 3. Intended User
James, 29, knows he scrolls too much. His mood tanks by evening, but he's never connected it to his phone use. He wants proof that his intuition is right—that screens are part of why he feels worse. Not to shame himself, but to understand. To see the pattern. Then to choose differently.

## 4. Behavioral Thesis
**What you can see, you can change.** Addiction thrives in invisibility. A daily mood + screen time journal exposes the loop: high screen time correlates with lower mood. This isn't blame. It's clarity. Users who *see* the pattern are more likely to experiment with changing it than those who only suspect it.

The thesis: **Visibility precedes choice. Pattern recognition precedes behavior change.**

## 5. Dignity Clause
This tool respects the user by:
- **Never shaming screen time.** High numbers don't trigger guilt messages. They're just data.
- **Never suggesting "the answer" is to quit screens.** Screens aren't evil. The tool shows the relationship, not a prescription.
- **Being honest about causation.** The graph shows correlation, not proof that screens *cause* bad mood. Other factors matter too.
- **Allowing context.** User can note "Rainy day," "Work deadline," "Great news" to add nuance.
- **Remaining private.** No comparisons. No social features. No shame.

## 6. Refusal Clause
This tool will intentionally NOT:
- Shame users with high screen time numbers
- Suggest quitting screens entirely
- Display "optimal" or "healthy" thresholds that create targets
- Use shame language ("You're addicted," "You're wasting time")
- Allow social sharing or comparison
- Create strict rules or judgments
- Become a blocking/limiting tool (it observes, doesn't restrict)

## 7. Atmospheric Tone
**Clear. Honest. Non-judgmental. Curious.**

The interface should feel like a scientist's notebook, not a therapist's judgment. When showing data, the tone is observational: "Look at this pattern. What do you notice?" Not: "You should change this."

The graph is the main character—visual, obvious, undeniable. The tool gets out of the way and lets the data speak.

---

## Design Principle
**Addiction loses power when exposed. Not through shame, but through clarity. Show the pattern. Trust the user to decide.**

Design for that.
