# Tool Intent Statement: Habit Garden

## 1. Core Function
A digital garden that grows based on completed daily habits. Each time a user completes a chosen habit, the garden becomes slightly more alive—plants appear, colors deepen, flowers bloom. The garden is the visual embodiment of consistency. It doesn't measure streaks. It doesn't compare. It simply shows: *this is what your practice looks like*.

## 2. Human Context
Habits are often abstract. "I meditated today." "I wrote 500 words." These achievements dissolve. There's no residue. No proof that the effort accumulates. The nervous system doesn't feel the weight of consistency because there's nowhere to *see* it.

A garden is different. Gardens are cumulative. Dead leaves become soil. Small plants become trees. The eye can track change across weeks. The body recognizes: *I made this. I'm making something.*

This tool enters the moment when someone wants to see their practice grow.

## 3. Intended User
Marcus, 34, tries to build habits but forgets why they matter. Meditation, writing, moving his body—all feel disconnected. They happen, then they're gone. He wants something that *accumulates visibly*. Not points. Not badges. A garden. Something alive that reflects what he's doing.

## 4. Behavioral Thesis
**Behavior becomes concrete through visibility.** Abstract habits (consistency, discipline) become tangible when they produce *something real*. A garden is a feedback loop that rewards not speed or intensity, but *presence over time*. The user learns that small, repeated actions build worlds.

The thesis: **Consistency compounds. Gardens prove it.**

## 5. Dignity Clause
This tool respects the user by:
- **Never shaming missed days.** The garden doesn't reset. Skipped days don't erase progress. The user starts again without penalty.
- **Never forcing comparison.** No leaderboards, no social features, no "see how you stack up." This is private practice.
- **Never rushing growth.** The garden grows at a human pace—slowly enough to feel earned, fast enough to be tangible.
- **Never tracking obsessively.** One simple question per day: "Did you practice?" Binary. Clean. Then the garden responds.
- **Never shaming the habit itself.** If someone chooses a hard habit, a small gesture still counts. The garden doesn't measure intensity—only shows up.

## 6. Refusal Clause
This tool will intentionally NOT:
- Track "streaks" or require consecutive days
- Punish missed days (no wilting, no death mechanics)
- Allow social sharing or comparison
- Display statistics, percentages, or metrics
- Create a sense of obligation or pressure
- Evolve beyond a simple, single garden
- Suggest the user needs to do more

## 7. Atmospheric Tone
**Alive. Patient. Generative.**

The interface should feel like tending, not achieving. When a user logs a habit, the garden responds gently—a new plant appears, a color shifts, flowers open. Not explosively. Not with celebration. With quiet acknowledgment: *you were here. You did the thing.*

The visual should be organic—hand-drawn quality if possible, or at least alive-feeling. Natural colors. Seasons might shift slowly. Growth should feel inevitable but patient, like actual gardens.

To use it should feel like stepping outside to check on what's growing.

---

## Design Principle
**Gardens don't rush. They don't compare. They just grow. Users who tend them learn that small, consistent actions create beauty.**

This is not a productivity app. This is a metaphor made visible. The user's habit becomes the gardener. The garden becomes proof that gardeners exist.

Design for that.
