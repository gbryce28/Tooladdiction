# Tool Intent Statement: Breath Anchor

## 1. Core Function
A simple timer that prompts the user to take 6 consecutive 6-second deep breaths every 10 minutes. Nothing more. No data collection. No gamification. No tracking streak. Just a gentle bell, a clear visual, and three minutes of structured breathing.

## 2. Human Context
Screens compress our breath. Anxiety lives in the chest. Distraction is fragmented attention that accumulates. A person scrolling, coding, working—attention scattered across tasks—doesn't notice they're shallow-breathing. The nervous system tightens. An hour passes. Three hours. The body registers stress as normal.

This tool enters that moment: not during crisis, but in the quiet drift. It offers a tiny island of intentionality. A small refusal to let the day compress you.

## 3. Intended User
Emma, 28, a designer who codes. She notices she holds her breath during focus. Her shoulders live at her ears. By evening, her neck aches and she's exhausted. She's not broken. She doesn't have a "breathing problem." She has a life that moves fast, and her body pays the price. She wants to notice. To reset. To feel less mechanical by day's end.

## 4. Behavioral Thesis
Behavior changes through *interruption*, not punishment. The tool doesn't shame shallow breathing. It doesn't gamify wellness. It simply breaks the rhythm at regular intervals and offers a clear alternative: pause, breathe, reset. The user learns through felt experience that three minutes changes how the next ten feel. No numbers. No achievement. Just sensation.

The thesis: **Awareness precedes change. Rhythm precedes awareness.**

## 5. Dignity Clause
This tool respects the user by:
- **Never tracking or recording.** No data about breathing patterns. No hidden surveillance.
- **Never shaming.** No messages like "you've been holding your breath" or "breathe better." Just an invitation.
- **Never demanding.** The timer is dismissible. The user can skip any session. Control remains theirs.
- **Never exploiting attention.** No notifications that compete. No sounds that alarm. A soft signal that respects the space it enters.
- **Never pathologizing.** This is not for anxiety disorder. This is for humans living in accelerated systems who sometimes forget to breathe.

## 6. Refusal Clause
This tool will intentionally NOT:
- Track streaks, minutes breathed, or any metric of compliance
- Display motivational language or progress bars
- Send push notifications or interruptions outside the app
- Collect personal data or behavioral patterns
- Suggest the user has a problem that needs solving
- Become a meditation app with courses, lessons, or hierarchy
- Compare the user to others or create communities
- Evolve into something "more useful"—it should stay focused and small

## 7. Atmospheric Tone
**Quiet. Reliable. Unobtrusive.**

The interface should feel like a trusted object: a watch that keeps time, a candle that holds light. Not urgent. Not friendly-corporate. Not clinical. A presence that has earned the right to suggest a pause through consistency and restraint.

The visual should be calm. The color palette muted. The typography clear and simple. The breathing prompt explicit without being instructive—show, don't tell. When the timer completes, let it fade gently. The ritual ends; the day continues.

To use it should feel less like *using an app* and more like *consulting an instrument*.

---

## Design Principle
**Interfaces are rituals. Rituals shape behavior. Behavior shapes identity.**

This tool believes that a 10-minute cycle, repeated across days, becomes a felt practice. It becomes muscle memory. It becomes the difference between a day that happens to you and a day you anchor yourself in.

Design for that.
